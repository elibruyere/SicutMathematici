English translation

Just as mathematicians consider figures abstracted from bodies, so I, in dealing with law, have withdrawn my mind from every particular fact.

Syntactic analysis

Sicut ... ita ...
Correlative comparative construction: just as ... so ... .
mathematici figuras a corporibus semotas considerant
Comparative clause. Mathematici is the subject, figuras the direct object, and semotas a perfect passive participle agreeing with figuras. A corporibus expresses separation.
me ... abduxisse animum
Accusative and infinitive construction (ACI): me is the subject, abduxisse the perfect active infinitive, and animum its direct object. The supplied excerpt does not contain the governing finite verb of this indirect statement. The English translation renders the infinitive as a finite verb for readability.
in iure tractando
Gerundive construction: in governs the ablative, and tractando agrees with iure. The phrase means in dealing with law, not law that must be dealt with.
ab omni singulari facto
Ablative of separation governed by ab; omni and singulari modify facto.

Philosophical and methodological significance

Grotius compares legal inquiry with mathematical abstraction. Just as mathematicians consider geometrical figures independently of physical bodies, the jurist abstracts from particular facts in order to examine general legal principles. The analogy concerns method; it does not by itself establish that legal reasoning has the same demonstrative certainty as mathematics.
